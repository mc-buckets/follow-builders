*Unsupervised Learning — Ep 88: Unpacking DeepMind's Quest for SuperIntelligence with Demis Hassabis' Biographer*

_The Takeaway: Demis Hassabis has always known AI is dangerous — but as the head of one lab, even a Nobel Prize winner can't stop a race that only governments can solve._

Sebastian Mallaby, author of _The Infinity Machine_, spent over 30 hours with DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis to write the first serious biography of the man behind AlphaGo, AlphaFold, and one of the most consequential AI labs in the world. The result is a portrait of a scientist who is simultaneously the most credentialed figure in the AI race and, paradoxically, the least famous — his publisher couldn't put him on the book cover because too few people would recognize him.

The most striking revelation isn't about strategy. It's about Hassabis's inner life. Mallaby describes him banging the table in their pub sessions, asking why atoms form a solid table, why a laptop made of sand and copper can think — and arriving somewhere unexpected: "Maybe if we approach science the right way, we understand more about nature, we will be getting closer to something that we could perhaps call God." For Hassabis, building superintelligence is a quasi-spiritual quest. Which may explain why he keeps pushing forward despite understanding the risks better than almost anyone.

On strategy, Mallaby is blunt: DeepMind's tendency to make multiple bets rather than concentrated ones has cost it. The ChatGPT moment went to OpenAI. The coding agent moment went to Anthropic. DeepMind makes world-class science but repeatedly fails to productize. Anthropic's low churn and intense identity under Dario Amodei is the counterexample — concentrated belief in one direction produces loyalty that diversified labs can't replicate.

Mallaby puts roughly 50/50 odds on OpenAI "failing" — meaning selling itself at a discount to a hyperscaler — by next summer. Not because the tech is bad, but because competing against Google's unlimited capital is structurally brutal for any startup.

The spin-out story is wild and underreported: a secret plan called Project Mario, Reid Hoffman's pledge of $1 billion to finance independence, and Hassabis using it as negotiating leverage against Google — without ever explicitly wielding it, and ultimately never exercising it. Mallaby's read: sometimes that's naivete. Sometimes it's a Nobel Prize.
