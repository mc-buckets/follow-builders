*AI & I by Every — "The Inside Story of the 50 Who Built Modern AI With Kevin Roose"*

Kevin Roose — bestselling author and co-host of the new NPR podcast Machine Gods — spent a year interviewing 150 people inside the AI labs to write the canonical account of how modern AI was built. His conclusion: we're not doomed, but we have real needles to thread, and the choices we make right now matter enormously.

*The Takeaway:* Roose used a "Council of Claudes" running in Claude Code to review every chapter of his book — each agent assigned a different editorial persona — and says the experience demonstrated just how much the tools have flipped: "A couple years ago, we had to fact check the AIs, and now the AIs fact check me."

His most useful mental model for navigating AI is the *weightlifting vs. forklifting* framework. Weightlifting tasks are those where the struggle is the point — parenting, creative writing, building judgment. Forklifting tasks are ones where you just need to move something heavy from A to B, and AI should do it. The mistake, he says, is mixing them up: "If you do, you end up using the forklift to lift your weights, you don't get any stronger."

For organizing his sprawling research, Roose leaned heavily on NotebookLM — dumping all source material in and querying it to find connections across 150+ interviews. He's currently running three or four agents simultaneously across work and personal admin.

The book's most surprising archival find: Dario Amodei's 2017 internal memo at OpenAI called "the big blob of compute hypothesis," which predicted that training massive unstructured compute on general tasks (like next-word prediction) would yield general intelligence — years before large language models existed. Roose published the first 10 pages on his Substack.

Machine Gods, his new podcast with Casey Newton, publishes twice a week in partnership with NPR and already scored a major interview: two of the three safety researchers fired from OpenAI over disputes about external auditor access.
