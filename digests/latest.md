*No Priors: Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein*

_The Takeaway:_ Enterprise data that's been sitting locked in dusty backup systems is suddenly the most valuable asset a company owns — and the threat to that data is no longer just human hackers, it's the AI agents companies are building themselves.

Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein, co-founders of EON, come from CloudEndure (acquired by AWS) and have spent years watching enterprises wrestle with their data. Their core insight: every company has decades of data scattered across siloed systems, but it's been effectively frozen — too expensive to extract, too unstructured to use, too risky to share across teams. AI changed the calculus entirely.

When AI models need training data, enterprise data stops being a cost center and becomes a moat. The Spirit Airlines bankruptcy illustrated this vividly — Google and Mercor both bid on the airline's dataset, not its planes. As Stein put it: _"The most valuable thing that you have is actually your data. And this data is gold."_

EON's approach is to build a new data foundation in the cloud that maps, classifies, and ingests data from across an organization's environment — without disrupting production systems or accidentally surfacing sensitive information to the wrong teams. The goal is to let AI models query this unified layer rather than forcing data engineers to build bespoke pipelines for every new use case.

The security angle is the sleeper insight: AI agents with legitimate credentials can now cause the same damage as ransomware attacks — dropping tables, leaking data — but at far greater velocity. Ehrlich notes that 6 months ago nobody was talking about this; today nearly every enterprise leader he meets has either experienced it or lives in fear of it. _"We need to assume breach, whether it's malicious or not."_

The cloud migration era required years of careful planning. The AI era is moving so fast that companies are paralyzed — not from lack of interest, but from loss of control. EON is betting that giving enterprises visibility and governance over their own data unlocks the AI adoption that's currently stuck.
