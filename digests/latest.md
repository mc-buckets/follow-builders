*AI & I by Every — "The AI Workflows Behind Every's Consulting Team"*

*The Takeaway:* Real software beats vibe-coded software — but only when you're honest about what you can actually maintain.

Natalia, head of consulting at Every, has one of the more grounded perspectives on what AI can and can't do for operators. She manages Claudie, Every's internal AI agent employee that handles CRM updates, LinkedIn and Twitter posts, and now self-evaluates its own performance on a loop. Claudie is working. And yet Natalia just hired a human ops person anyway.

Her reason is clarifying: AI is exceptional at executing against a standard operating procedure, but it still needs constant oversight for taste and judgment, and it can't substitute for human relationships. The human hire isn't replacing Claudie — they're analyzing the data Claudie surfaces and leading the conversations that data enables.

The most counterintuitive move she made: buying a real CRM (Atiyo) instead of continuing to vibe-code one. "In the era of AI, you can build anything. The question is, should you build and maintain whatever you actually build?" A sales pipeline has hundreds of edge cases that compound over time — real software compiles those rules deterministically, and AI won't one-shot all of them upfront. She's now a convert to writing PRDs before building anything.

The tool she won't stop talking about: Codex. Coming from a non-technical background and already comfortable with Claude Code, she was initially reluctant — but once she used it, she built a fully functioning email triage app (trained on 150 of her own emails, writes in her voice, routes to Asana or client markdown files with one click), and ran a 13-hour overnight Codex session to populate her new CRM from months of email and call notes. She woke up to weeks of work done. "My email knows what's going on more than I do."

For executives wondering where to start: give your existing KPIs and OKRs to AI as shared context, identify what only humans should do, and standardize one simple task before trying to rebuild everything. "The biggest mistake is wanting to be AI-first overnight. It starts with writing down how you do a single thing really well."
