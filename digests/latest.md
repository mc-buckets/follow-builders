*Andrej Karpathy* (ex-Director of AI at Tesla, founding team at OpenAI)

Karpathy made a compelling case for asking LLMs to format their responses as HTML — just append "structure your response as HTML" to your query and open the file in a browser. He frames this as part of a broader evolution in how humans and AI exchange information: audio is the preferred human _input_ to AI, while vision (images, animations, video) is AI's preferred _output_ mode. He sketches a progression from raw text → markdown → HTML → eventual "interactive neural videos" generated by diffusion models. He also notes current interaction is missing gestural/pointing input — the ability to indicate things on your screen the way you would with someone physically next to you.

*No Priors — Amex Global Business Travel: The World's First AI Take Private with Long Lake CEO Alexander Taubman*

_The Takeaway:_ AI-native private equity is no longer theoretical — Long Lake just put $6.3B behind the thesis that buying and operating services companies with a shared AI platform beats selling software to them.

Alexander Taubman, cofounder and CEO of Long Lake Management, has spent the past few years building what may be the most consequential AI application most people haven't heard of: an AI-driven buyout firm. Long Lake acquires services businesses — HOA management, HR services, specialty tax, and now corporate travel — deploys their proprietary "Nexus" AI platform, and compounds from there. With the announced $6.3B acquisition of American Express Global Business Travel (a 111-year-old company originally founded to help Travelex customers evacuate Europe during WWI), Long Lake is making their biggest bet yet.

What makes the model distinctive is what it's _not_: this is not traditional PE cost-cutting. Taubman's core thesis is that AI makes employees more productive, productive employees stay longer, and happier teams deliver better customer outcomes — a compounding flywheel. In their HOA business, annual growth went from 0-5% to 20%+ after deploying Nexus.

The counterintuitive insight: labor-intensive services businesses face a brutal marginal tax on growth — every 20% revenue increase requires hiring 20% more people, and you keep maybe 20 cents of each incremental dollar after labor costs. AI breaks that ceiling entirely, letting the business grow with software-style incremental margins.

_"Our view of AI is it's incredibly positive sum. We actually think AI makes people more productive. And we have more productive people. You want more of them."_

Taubman also argues enterprise AI is only ~1% penetrated in real use cases today, and that the hardest part isn't the model — it's change management. The reason Long Lake buys companies instead of selling them software: ownership creates the deep alignment needed to actually change how people work, not just what tools they have access to. Their engineering team is embedded across 20 states, sitting with frontline employees to understand pain points and build solutions inside Nexus.
