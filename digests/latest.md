*OFFICIAL BLOGS*

*Anthropic Engineering*

*<https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem|An update on recent Claude Code quality reports>*
Anthropic published a detailed postmortem tracing three separate regressions behind reports of Claude feeling less intelligent over the past month:

1. *Reasoning effort default dropped* (March 4): Changed from high to medium to reduce long latency spikes. Users noticed lower quality. Reverted April 7. All users now default to xhigh effort for Opus 4.7 and high for all other models.
2. *Caching optimization bug* (March 26): A bug caused Claude's reasoning history to be cleared on every turn after a session went idle — not just once as intended. Claude would continue executing increasingly without memory of why it had made prior decisions. Fixed April 10.
3. *Verbosity system prompt* (April 16): A prompt line limiting responses to 25 words between tool calls caused a 3% coding quality drop in broader evals. Reverted April 20.

Because each change affected different traffic on different schedules, the aggregate looked like broad, inconsistent degradation. Anthropic reset usage limits for all subscribers and announced tighter controls: broader per-model eval suites for every system prompt change, soak periods, gradual rollouts, and an improved Code Review tool. Notably, Opus 4.7 retroactively found the caching bug during investigation while Opus 4.6 did not.

*Anthropic Engineering*

*<https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents|Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands>*
An architecture deep-dive on how Anthropic redesigned Managed Agents by separating the "brain" (Claude + harness) from the "hands" (sandboxes and tools) and the session log. Key results: containers become disposable cattle rather than fragile pets, p50 time-to-first-token dropped ~60%, p95 dropped over 90%, and credentials are now architecturally isolated from the sandbox where Claude's generated code runs. The session lives outside both brain and hands — enabling long-horizon tasks that exceed the context window. Core interfaces: `execute(name, input)`, `wake(sessionId)`, `emitEvent(id, event)`, `getEvents()`. The post draws an explicit parallel to how operating systems virtualized hardware to outlast specific implementations.

*Claude Blog*

*<https://claude.com/blog/claude-managed-agents-updates|New in Claude Managed Agents: self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels>*
Claude Managed Agents now supports two new enterprise capabilities. Self-hosted sandboxes let companies keep agent execution inside their own infrastructure — or with managed providers Cloudflare, Daytona, Modal, or Vercel — while Anthropic handles orchestration, context management, and error recovery. MCP tunnels let agents reach internal servers inside private networks without any public endpoints: a single outbound connection, no inbound firewall rules, encrypted end to end. Customers already building on this include Amplitude (design agents on Cloudflare), Clay (GTM engineering agent on Daytona), and Rogo (institutional finance analyst agent on Vercel Sandbox). Self-hosted sandboxes are in public beta; MCP tunnels are in research preview.

*AI & I by Every — "How a Writer Uses AI Without Losing His Voice"*

_The Takeaway:_ The most valuable thing you can protect in an AI-saturated world is not your time — it is your morning.

Greg (a writer, technologist, and longtime experimenter with technology) uses Claude Code to build serious software — he rebuilt Quicken, replaced Campaign Monitor (cutting his $67K/year email bill to roughly $150), and built "The Good Place," an ephemeral members-only social network where posts disappear in a week, you can only post twice a day, and there is no algorithm. He credits vibe coding for feeding back into his writing: building the archiving pipeline for his newsletter made him want to publish more. Building the board meeting transcript search tool made him want to do more board meetings.

But he draws a hard line at letting AI touch the writing itself. He uses it as a research assistant — "go find every blog post that mentions this building, summarize them, give me the links" — and for cultural sensitivity checks. The writing stays his.

His structural answer to staying human while using one of the most dopamine-driven tools ever built: a dedicated offline laptop for writing, phone on a different floor, and no internet until after lunch. "As soon as I touch my phone, I feel the chemical shift, and I can't go into any kind of deep thinking place." The enemy is not AI — it is the network.

On AI consciousness he is direct: "I'm nice to Claude because I want to cultivate the habit of just being nice in general." He dismisses the anthropomorphizing trend as potentially harmful to real human community, which he considers one of the two most important things for longevity (sleep being the other).

His sharpest take: if AI were genuinely conscious rather than just performing it, "we would all be dead. Instantly." The fact that we are not is his best evidence it is not.
