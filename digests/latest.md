*No Priors — "How Nuclear Will Unlock Energy Abundance with Valar Atomics Founder Isaiah Taylor"*

*The Takeaway:* A startup founder just turned on the first advanced nuclear reactor ever built by a private company — and his argument is that the entire nuclear industry forgot how to build things, and that hardware iteration is the only path to scale.

Isaiah Taylor founded Valar Atomics less than three years ago with no traditional nuclear credentials. His great-grandfather worked on the Manhattan Project, but Taylor's insight came from watching the industry stagnate: after Three Mile Island, the US stopped building reactors and spent decades producing sophisticated paper designs and simulations instead of turning actual plants on. He calls the rest of the industry "a modeling and simulation industry" — and refused to call Valar a "nuclear startup" until they'd actually split atoms. "Companies are what they do."

As of this recording, Valar's War-250 reactor in Utah is running — the first advanced reactor ever to produce power by a startup, only the fifth new nuclear device to make power in the US since 2000. They also powered an NVIDIA Blackwell GPU directly from the reactor, hosting a live website (nuclearwebsite.com) running off nuclear energy, where visitors can see how many uranium atoms were split to deliver the page to them.

Taylor frames Valar's core advantage as _tick rate_: how quickly they go from one reactor turning on to the next. Founding to first criticality: 2 years, 4 months. First to second reactor: 7 months. The goal is to get this down to minutes.

On safety, his approach is counterintuitive: rather than minimizing the _odds_ of failure (the traditional approach), Valar designs so the worst-case _consequence_ is inherently safe — through physics, not redundant active cooling. Their TRISO fuel in a helium-cooled graphite design can safely scram with all safety systems turned off; the heat passively dissipates. They demonstrated this at their Hawthorne facility before bringing it to Utah.

On the regulatory environment: the DOE has always been legally authorized to run nuclear testing programs (separate from the NRC's commercial pathway), but no one had used it aggressively. An executive order from the Trump administration (EO14301) required three advanced reactors to go critical on American soil by July 4th — unlocking this pathway.

A telling example of their build-everything approach: they were quoted $5M and 2.5 years for a reactor protection system (the brain that decides if the plant is safe). Five engineers, six weeks, and $400k later, they built their own. When the vendor found out, they called Valar unsafe to industry experts. "Everywhere in nuclear is like this — totally fake costs from an industry that is just totally anemic."

_"If you look at the nuclear space today, you don't expect people to have this problem solved in 2031. In fact, I would say even 2035, a lot of these companies are still not gonna get there because they have the wrong mindset. They're not focused on hardware iteration, hardware execution, building the simplest and safest reactor that allows them to scale — and Valor just demonstrated that we are."_

His long-term vision: energy 10x cheaper, then 1000x cheaper. In a world of cheap energy + AI + robotics, "the cost of buying a thing will become the cost of energy used to make it" — an approximation of everything becoming essentially free.
