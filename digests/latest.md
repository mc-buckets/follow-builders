*No Priors — "Taking on ServiceNow: Coding Agents the Rest of Us with Serval Co-Founder and CEO Jake Stauch"*

*The Takeaway:* The enterprise AI opportunity isn't about replacing knowledge workers — it's about giving IT departments the same coding-agent superpowers that software engineers already have, wrapped in the guardrails that large enterprises actually require.

Jake Stauch, co-founder and CEO of Serval (2.5 years old, now 200 people after starting the year at 20), is building a direct attack on ServiceNow — the entrenched, expensive, and notoriously slow-to-build-on enterprise IT platform. His thesis from day one: code is the best way to express automation, drag-and-drop workflow builders are a painful middle ground for everyone, and AI coding agents would eventually make code-based automation accessible to anyone. Two years in, the bet has paid off.

The contrarian insight: IT departments in most companies *are* the engineering team. Most businesses don't have a software org — they have an IT org. Serval treats them as the capable builders they are, bringing SDLC concepts (dev/staging/prod, code review, audit trails) to teams that have never had them.

On the SaaS apocalypse debate, Stauch offers a nuanced view: point solutions will get eaten, but systems of record (ERP, HRIS, ITSM, CRM) will survive — just transformed. "All the companies I've talked to that tried to vibe-code these tools internally have discovered that enterprise software is quite expensive to build and maintain."

On background agents — the "ultimate moonshot" in enterprise software — his solution is incremental escalation: agents propose specific deterministic actions, humans approve them, and over time the agent's toolkit grows. Not magical autonomy, but a controlled ramp toward trust.

"Serval is the boundary. Serval is the guardrails. We are the way that a large enterprise with a lot of rules and approvals and process and politics can adopt these tools safely."
