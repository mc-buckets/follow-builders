*AI & I by Every (now: The Every Podcast) — "How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time"*

_The Takeaway:_ Sam Altman believes we're entering an AI-powered Renaissance — not an industrial revolution — and the proof is that humans are more central to the future than most people expected.

Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, sat down with Every's Dan Shipper at Dev Day, where OpenAI launched 22 products (up from 9 last year, and 4-5 the year before). Altman's framing for all of it: this moment is more Renaissance than Industrial Revolution. The Industrial Revolution created specialization that disconnected people from what made life good. The Renaissance reconnected people to human potential. His bet is that AI does the same.

The most surprising lesson from the AI coding era? There was massive latent demand no one predicted, and humans have remained at the center of everything — we care about the person behind the creation, not just the output. His example: when 10,000 agents solved a major math problem, most of the public conversation was about interpretation and the people involved, not the solution itself.

On "Dots" — OpenAI's new personal agent — Altman is a genuine convert. He used to love early mornings for deep work, but checking his phone for urgent issues contaminated his focus. Now his Dot (named Dottie) handles triage, and he gets the creative space back. During Dev Day itself, a teammate's Dot flagged a potential demo failure five minutes before the keynote. During the interview, his Dot flagged the one thing he actually needed to handle during a five-minute break.

_"It sounds small, but like a significant improvement to my life."_

On Ultrafast (OpenAI's fastest inference tier): Altman does all his prompting there and admits he has a hard time going back. The insight is about feedback loop speed — fast responses change how you think iteratively in a way that's "a wild difference." His projection: the economics of speed will follow the same relentless downward curve as model intelligence. "We'll deliver 8X [speed] at a great price in the not distant future."

He also said he wants to build and ship a plugin or extension himself — not as a side project, but as something real, over the threshold.
