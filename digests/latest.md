*Unsupervised Learning — Ep 90: AI Pioneer Jürgen Schmidhuber on the State of AI Today*

_The Takeaway:_ The AI CapEx boom is badly mispriced, real AGI requires physical robots not screen-based AI, and the companies racing to own recursive self-improvement probably won't — because the foundational ideas come from academia and can't be moated.

Jürgen Schmidhuber pioneered LSTM networks, meta-learning, and recursive self-improvement research stretching back to the 1980s. He offers a sobering and contrarian read on today's moment — and unlike most commentators, he isn't surprised by any of it.

On the CapEx bubble: *"The guys who are investing a thousand billion dollars into GPUs for data centers today — within the next five years, they are going to lose $900,000,000,000. There's no business model that can recuperate that loss."* His logic: compute gets 10x cheaper roughly every five years. Companies buying hardware at today's prices are pre-purchasing something that will be obsolete. Open source models relentlessly pressure pricing, leaving no margin to recoup.

On recursive self-improvement as a moat: He doesn't buy it. The core ideas all came from small academic labs, not big companies. "There are all these other PhD students out there who are super excited about self-improvement, and there's no way to keep a little advantage there for long enough to make a lot of money."

On what today's LLMs are missing: They're "super biased towards humans" because they're trained on human-generated web data — a tiny fraction of all possible data. True AI scientists should generate their own training data through curiosity and experimentation, the way babies learn by predicting the consequences of their actions, not by downloading the web.

On AI safety: He's notably less worried than peers. He argues that alignment to a single objective is naive, that sufficiently intelligent AI scientists will be motivated to _protect_ life because they'll find it fascinating, and that today's AI-vs-AI drone warfare already shows how irrelevant top-down alignment frameworks are in practice.

On robots: Hardware remains the binding constraint. "You can't have AGI without hardware like that. You can't have AGI just behind the screen." Human-comparable robot dexterity is still likely decades away.
