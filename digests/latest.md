*AI & I by Every* — _A New Vision for Human Work After Automation_

*The Takeaway:* The aggregate view of AI — capability curves, benchmarks, models beating humans at expert tasks — misses what actually determines value: the unique perspective and responsibility each person brings to the model.

Every CEO Dan Shipper closed Every's first-ever conference (Thesis: 2027, a full day in Brooklyn with speakers from Anthropic, OpenAI, Notion, Lovable, and more) with this argument. His starting point: OpenAI's GDPval benchmark shows AI beating human experts ~83% of the time in 2026, up from 12.4% in 2024. That's the "aggregate" — extrapolate the trend line and human work appears to be going to zero.

But Shipper thinks the aggregate is misleading because it freezes humans in time to make the measurement work. The "intimate" lens — zooming in on one real situation — tells a different story. In a single week, in the margins of his calendar, he analyzed their quarterly customer survey, built a video game for the all-hands, spotted a team-sales opportunity, vibe-coded a CRM, and started cold outreach. Work that would have required five specialists and $200K in 2020.

Here's what made it interesting: when he shared the same survey data and the same AI tools with the full team, every person found something different and important — based on what they were responsible for and what they cared about. No one's output looked like the default Claude response.

_"Generation is really cheap, but ranking is hard."_ In one internal experiment, Claude could predict what Dan would say in Slack — but only when given 400 simultaneous attempts. When asked to rank its own answers, Dan's response landed in the top 10% only about 60% of the time.

His conclusion: as models improve, they raise the floor for everyone — the default answer becomes the baseline, not the differentiator. What's valuable is using your responsibility for a particular part of the world, and your perspective, to push past that floor. And that's why he thinks we should stop measuring AI by consensus-validated benchmarks (math, code) and start asking: _how good is it at doing my work, from my perspective?_
