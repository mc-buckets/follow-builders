*The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "The Biggest AI Deployment Nobody Talks About | Samsara CEO Sanjit Biswas"*

*The Takeaway:* Physical operations — trucking, construction, utilities — represent 40–50% of global GDP and are now the frontier of AI deployment, powered by proprietary sensor data that no model can scrape from the internet.

Sanjit Biswas co-founded Meraki (sold to Cisco) before building Samsara, now a $20B company at $2B ARR growing 30% and profitable. The platform monitors millions of commercial vehicles covering 99% of US roads daily, generating 25 trillion data points per year — and the company claims to have helped prevent ~380,000 road accidents last year alone.

Biswas draws the core distinction between physical and digital AI: "These are not the tokens you're gonna find online. Like, you can't crawl Reddit and find out about what happened on a construction site." Proprietary operational data — dashcam video, GPS pings, engine diagnostics, asset trackers — is Samsara's moat, not any single model.

At their Beyond 2026 conference, Samsara launched *Agent Studio*, moving beyond simple chatbots toward autonomous operations. The flagship demo: a warranty agent that reads service manuals, cross-references OEM warranty agreements, opens a work order, and checks the rest of the fleet for the same issue — automating 1–2 hours of human labor down to under a minute.

On the physical automation timeline: Biswas sees near-term AI handling repetitive physical tasks (grading roads overnight, long-haul routing between fixed points) while humans manage exception-heavy work. He's more cautious on autonomous trucking than robotaxis given the long tail of specialized vehicles and messy job sites. And dashcams, once purely safety tools, are becoming two-way AI interfaces — briefing drivers, coaching them in real time, connecting them to dispatch.

One energy utility customer put the macro in sharp relief: over 125 years they built a certain amount of grid capacity. In the next 5 years, they'll triple it. 90% of that new demand is data centers.
