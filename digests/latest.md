*AI & I by Every — "How Hugging Face's Investor Spots Winners"*
<https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL|Watch the episode>

*The Takeaway:* Back technical founders who pivot on technology — not market expertise — because they'll find product-market fit wherever the tech leads them, not just where they started.

Matt Hartman, founder and managing partner at Factorial Capital, wrote the first check into Hugging Face when it was still a chatbot for teenagers. NVIDIA bought Hugging Face for $13B last month. Hartman spent nearly a decade at Betaworks before launching Factorial — a solo-GP fund with a network of technical venture partners including Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue and Venmo co-founder Iqram Magdon-Ismail.

His investing thesis centers on the "newly technically possible." He reads papers, tinkers obsessively, and looks for founders with _earned insights_ — ideas that only a small number of people were positioned to discover. "If only 10 people in the world have come across this because it's a particular area of expertise, you're going to have more time and you've already spent a lot of time learning that problem."

Hartman's "pivot foot" framework: technical founders can pivot toward wherever the technology goes; domain-expert founders are constrained to pivot within their vertical. The companies he's been most involved with succeed when the pivot is about the technology, not market expertise.

On speed of iteration in the AI era: coding cycles that took two months now take two days. "The person whose job is to understand the user and translate that into the next version — that job's value is going up exponentially because of the speed of iteration." He sees this as the most valuable skill in building companies today.

He also shipped Ghost Pepper — a local, open-source voice-to-text tool using Hugging Face models that hit #1 on Hacker News — as a way to stay sharp on what's newly buildable. The maintenance burden taught him to appreciate SaaS products that keep iterating. "What you're paying for is them making sure it stays a great product."

Current investment themes: developer tools for a new kind of developer, non-language frontier models (robotics, science, weather), and application-layer companies that can go full-stack — balancing their own model capabilities with frontier API access.
