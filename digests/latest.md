*AI & I by Every — "How Anthropic Uses Claude Fable 5 With Mike Krieger"*

Mike Krieger — cofounder of Instagram and now head of Anthropic Labs — has been using Claude Fable 5 for months before its public release, and the biggest shift he describes isn't capability: it's workflow.

The old mental model of AI coding — decompose tasks carefully, prompt iteratively, stay in the loop — is largely obsolete with Fable. Krieger now spends more time upfront on architectural planning conversations, uses Fable to generate alignment artifacts (HTML diagrams, markdown docs) before execution, then steps away. "I wish Claude a good night, set it up on a pretty complex task...and wake up to it usually done by two in the morning." When an external service went down mid-task overnight, Fable scaffolded a working mock backend, documented the workaround, and flagged the item for follow-up — without being prompted.

The most concrete example of what this enables: Krieger ran a full Python-to-TypeScript codebase port over a single weekend using Dynamic Workflows. Fable designed the migration plan, executed module by module, ran adversarial tests, documented exceptions, and delivered a clean TypeScript/Bun port. He couldn't have done it with previous models at that success rate or without the scaffolding workflows provide. On verification, he's developed a new discipline: every pull request should have an attached screenshot or video gallery. He's been feeding Fable raw video captures of UI changes so it can catch animation jank that static screenshots miss — a technique he considers significantly underexplored.

On whether software engineering is "over": no, but dramatically changed. The PM/engineer split is collapsing, and the volume of parallelism required has exploded. What remains distinctly human is ownership, contextual judgment, and knowing when to bounce a server versus rearchitect a system. The most memorable signal: a recruiter at Anthropic who gained access to a Fable-powered internal tool said it was "the first time in my life where the thing in my head and the thing that exists in the world are right next to each other."
